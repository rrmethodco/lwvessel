The question you asked

Is there a common thread of size or space limitation? No — not size. "Guest Count Exceeds Room Capacity" is recorded on 15 of 947 diagnosed losses, about 3.7% of lost value. The 13th Floor has hosted 250 guests. Room size is not what is costing this business.

But there is a space thread

It is availability, not size — and it is seasonal. 125 inquiries worth roughly $1.5M were turned away as Date Unavailable, and 84% of them fall between May and October, precisely when the wedding book occupies the 13th Floor. September alone lost 28 dates against 5 wins.

Corporate converts one inquiry in four and a half. Social converts one in thirty-four. These are not the same business and should not be managed the same way. Social's volume is a low-intent web stream — 1,393 inquiries against 71 booked events all year, with 417 of them not events at all.

A caution on sizing the loss

Valuing all 53,693 lost social covers at the booked rate would imply $10.7M of lost social business. That figure is not defensible and is not used here. A 2.9% win rate on an unqualified web stream reflects inquiry quality, not sales failure. Only the diagnosed, addressable buckets are valued in this report — dates we actively turned away, and parties we told were too large.

Finding one — the direct answer

Size is not the constraint

Capacity is cited on 15 of 947 losses (1.6% by count, 3.7% by value) — one corporate event and fourteen social. Even among parties of 150 or more, where you would expect a wall, capacity explains only 6 of 102 losses; 75 of them are simply No Response. And the room is bigger than the lost business: the 13th Floor has hosted 250 guests in a single event, against a largest lost social party of 400 and a median lost party of 22.

Finding two — the real space thread

Availability, and it collides with wedding season

125 qualified inquiries were turned away for want of a date — 7,495 covers, roughly $1.5M at booked per-cover rates. They are not spread across the year. 84% land between May and October, the exact months the wedding book fills the 13th Floor. This is the one place where space genuinely costs money, and it is a calendar problem rather than a room-size problem.

Section three

What to Do

Ordered by recoverable value. Nothing here requires a bigger room.

1

Answer the corporate inquiries. 35 of 68 corporate losses are silence.

1,853 covers — roughly $430,000 — lost with no competitive reason, no price objection and no calendar conflict. Corporate converts at 22% when worked and is the segment where a reply reliably becomes a booking.

Recovers up to $430,000 · 1,853 covers

2

Sell the shoulder date before refusing the peak one.

125 inquiries were turned away for a date, 84% of them May–October against a full wedding book. None of them appear to have been offered an alternative date, a different room, or a daytime slot. A structured second-offer at the point of refusal is the difference between a lost inquiry and a Tuesday booking.

Addresses ~$1.5M of refused demand

3

Route restaurant reservations out of the events inbox.

429 of 1,500 inquiries — 29% — were guests wanting an ordinary table. They consume coordinator time and drag the reported win rate from 5.9% to 4.3%. Social is the worst affected: 417 of its 1,320 losses were never events.

Removes 29% of inquiry volume · corrects the win rate

4

Qualify social before working it, and stop reporting it alongside corporate.

Social runs 13× corporate's inquiry volume for 60% fewer booked covers, and reporting them as one pipeline hides corporate's real performance. Social needs a qualification gate — date, budget, headcount — not more follow-up capacity.

Protects corporate attention from social volume

5

Retire "Other" as a loss reason.

110 losses carrying 4,602 covers are recorded as Other — 12% of the diagnosed book with no cause attached. Anthology's taxonomy is the best in the portfolio; this gap is what stops it being fully actionable.

What changed since the first version

The original analysis drew from the Tripleseat API for location 22266, which returns two books under one label — Anthology's own events and the restaurants' private dining (Le Suprême, HIROKI-SAN) — and which ignores its location_id filter entirely, returning every venue on the account. Both faults inflated the earlier figures.

This revision is scoped to Anthology's own book using the Tripleseat events export, with per-cover values taken from booked 2026 business net of tax and gratuity ($232 corporate, $200 social). Corrected: corporate is 50 booked bookings, not 237; social 71, not 204.
