Findings
What the shop says about Vessel's group & events position
  • The 4-of-4 ranking is a process problem, not a hospitality problem. Vessel earned a "Definitely would buy" rating and the warmest rapport notes in the set — but made no proactive attempt to close. The winner (Courtyard) separated itself with a simple "Request a Contract" link.
  • Rate position is aggressive for the comp-set standing: $229 quoted ($219 after resistance) — second only to Canopy's $372, +38% over the Courtyard's $159 and +18% over the Kimpton's $185. Defensible only if the sales process and packaging carry it.
  • F&B minimum now set at $1,500, in line with the comp set ($1,000–$3,500). The BEO calculator treats minimums as the revenue floor on buyouts — a $0 quote on group receptions leaves margin on the table, so holding this floor matters.
  • Fee presentation: 25% (20% service + 5% special event fee) is top-of-set and itemized where every comp quotes one number — an unnecessary comparison-shopping snag.
  • Concessions are given away at the opening quote: waived attrition and comp internet against the set's standard 80% attrition — chips better held until a signed contract or rate hold.
  • Real value points exist to sell the premium: $950 rental is mid-pack (Kimpton $500, Canopy $1,500), and $20 self-parking is the cheapest guest cost in a valet-heavy set.
Recommended actions — for approval
Suggested changes to the Vessel group & events playbook
  • Add a closing step to the sales SOP: every proposal goes out with a contract link (or offer to hold the space) and a calendared follow-up within 48 hours — the zero-cost fix for the 4-of-4 ranking.
  • Hold the $1,500 group-event F&B minimum now in place (buyouts already carry $1,500 per the BEO file) so Vessel is never the property quoting $0 against a set at $1,000–$3,500.
  • Quote fees as a single inclusive 25% in proposals (itemize 20% + 5% only at contract) to match how the comp set presents.
  • Hold attrition waiver and comps as negotiation chips — open at the set-standard 80% attrition and concede only against a signed contract or rate hold.
  • Outside catering now restricted: not permitted, matching every comp in the set — protecting the F&B margin the minimum secures; allow only specialty items by contract exception.
  • Arm the rate premium with a value sheet: rental incl. 30-person reception, $20 self-parking vs valet-only comps, waived-attrition flexibility — the package that justifies $219 vs the Courtyard's $159.

Findings
Where Vessel sits in the waterfront F&B comp set
  • Vessel is the only venue publishing entry terms. $1,500 F&B minimum, $950 rental and a 25% service structure are all on the record; all four restaurant comps make the client call for a quote. Speed-and-clarity of pricing is a live differentiator, not a marketing line.
  • Scale splits the set in two. Rye Street’s Overlook (250 seated / 400 reception) and Nick’s large waterfront deck are the big-format players; Vessel and Copper Shark are intimate (≤115); Urbano is mid-size. Vessel’s natural peer is Copper Shark, and its opening is the ≤120-guest booking a 400-cap room makes feel empty.
  • Waterfront is table stakes here. Vessel, Rye Street and Nick’s are all on the water; Copper Shark (Locust Point) and Urbano (Peninsula) are near-water. The view alone doesn’t separate Vessel — exclusive full-buyout + transparent pricing does.
  • Catering lanes differ. Everyone is in-house / no-outside-catering, but Urbano uniquely pushes its own off-premise catering — a different competitor lane (they’ll cater the client’s space, not just host).
  • Brand asymmetry. Rye Street carries NoHo Hospitality (Andrew Carmellini) national equity and a 315-seat flagship; Vessel can’t out-brand it and shouldn’t try — it wins on intimacy, flexibility and Method Co. service.
Recommended actions — for approval
How Vessel should sell against the F&B set
  • Lead with published terms + a same-day quote. The entire restaurant comp set forces a “call for pricing” — be the easy yes with a number in hand before they’ve heard back from the others.
  • Sell the full-buyout exclusive vs. a restaurant’s corner. Against Copper Shark and Urbano’s semi-private rooms, Vessel gives the client the whole waterfront space — position exclusivity, not square footage.
  • Own the overflow lane below Rye Street’s Overlook. Target ≤120-guest events where a 250/400 room is oversized and impersonal — the sweet spot Vessel’s buyout actually fits.
  • Counter Urbano’s off-prem catering by owning the on-site experience they can’t replicate off the water.
  • Re-shop for hard terms. None of the four publish F&B minimums, rentals or service %; a quick phone/email mystery shop (as done for the hotel set) would close every “quote on request” gap in the matrix above.

Comp set: Rye Street Tavern (Baltimore Peninsula), Urbano / Urbano 116 (Baltimore Peninsula · Rye Street Market), Nick’s Fish House (2600 Insulator Dr, Middle Branch) and Copper Shark (921 E Fort Ave, Locust Point) — the South-Baltimore waterfront F&B venues most directly competing with Vessel for private events and buyouts.
